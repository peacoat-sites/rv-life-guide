# How RV Life Guide Is Written

RV Life Guide covers RV ownership and travel: buying and running costs, towing, maintenance, campgrounds, boondocking and full-time living. This page explains, plainly, how the articles are made.

## How articles are made

Articles on RV Life Guide are drafted with AI writing tools. Each draft starts from public reference sources on the topic, including those listed below, and from recent news when a topic is changing.

Before an article is published, a second automated pass reviews it and removes or rewrites anything it cannot stand behind: statistics, prices or dates it is not confident are correct, studies or organizations that may be misattributed, and personal stories presented as if they happened to the writer.

Articles are not reviewed by a certified RV technician before publication. Use them as background for a conversation with one, not as a substitute.

The site owner audits published articles in batches, merging pages that overlap, deleting weak ones and correcting errors.

## Who writes here

The bylines on RV Life Guide (Barbara Mitchell, Greg Hoffman, Julia Davidson, Sandra Park, Tony Reeves) are pen names, one for each area of coverage. They are not real individuals, they hold no professional licenses, and their profile images are illustrations, not photographs of real people.

## Sources we rely on

- [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/): Camping and park information
- [Bureau of Land Management](https://www.blm.gov/): Dispersed camping and public land
- [NHTSA](https://www.nhtsa.gov/): Towing and trailer safety
- [Federal Highway Administration](https://highways.dot.gov/): Roads and travel

Where an article depends on a specific figure, rule or study, it links to or names the source so you can check it.

## Limits of this site

Weight and towing figures in our articles and calculators are estimates. Always go by the ratings on your vehicle and hitch (GVWR, GCWR, tongue weight and hitch class) and your owner's manual, and check the towing and length rules in each state you drive through. Propane, electrical and structural repairs carry real risk; when in doubt, use a qualified RV technician.

## Corrections

If you find an error, please report it through the [contact page](/contact/). Reports go directly to the site owner, and confirmed errors are corrected in the article.

## How the site makes money

RV Life Guide may earn money from display advertising and from affiliate links, including Amazon links (as an Amazon Associate, the site earns from qualifying purchases). Advertisers and affiliate partners do not see or approve articles before they are published.
