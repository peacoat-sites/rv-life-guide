You sold your house, packed everything into a 36-foot motorhome, and you're ready to hit the road full time. Then someone asks: "So where do you *live*?" It's a question that stops a lot of new full-timers cold. The answer matters more than you'd think. Your domicile state determines your income tax burden, vehicle registration costs, health insurance options, and even your jury duty obligations. Pick the wrong state and you could owe thousands in unexpected taxes. Pick the right one and you might save $4,000 to $8,000 a year or more, depending on your income.

## What Domicile Actually Means (And Why It's Not the Same as Residency)

People use "domicile" and "residency" interchangeably, but they're legally distinct. Your residence is where you physically sleep. Your domicile is your permanent legal home, the place you intend to return to even if you're away for years. For full-time RVers, there's often no physical residence at all, which is exactly why choosing a domicile state is a real decision you have to make deliberately.

Every state has its own rules about what creates domicile. Generally, it requires obtaining a driver's license, registering your vehicle, and maintaining a mailing address in the state. Some states also want proof of banking, voter registration, or a notarized statement of intent. The bar varies, but meeting the basic requirements isn't usually the hard part. The hard part is choosing which state deserves that legal tie.

A few caveats upfront. Your previous home state doesn't automatically release you just because you drove away. If you owned a home in California, for example, and you're still receiving mail there, California may still consider you a resident and expect income tax. Formally establishing domicile elsewhere, and cutting ties with your old state, is a required step, not an optional one.

## The Big Three: South Dakota, Texas, and Florida

Three states dominate the RV domicile conversation, and for good reason. They're the most RV-friendly, they have established infrastructure for non-resident registration, and they've been doing this long enough that the process is well understood.

**South Dakota** is arguably the most popular choice among full-time RVers. There's no state income tax. Vehicle registration fees are reasonable, around $270 for a larger motorhome annually (varies by weight and type). The state allows you to establish domicile in a single day if you visit in person, and mail forwarding companies like America's Mailbox in Box Elder and Alternative Resources in Sioux Falls have processed thousands of RVer applications. You'll need a physical address for your driver's license and registration, which these services provide. South Dakota also has relatively lenient rules about how often you need to physically return to the state.

**Texas** is the other major contender. No state income tax either, and Texas has a large RV culture with mail forwarding services clustered around Livingston (home of the Escapees RV Club's headquarters). The Texas process requires a bit more documentation than South Dakota, and vehicle registration fees can run higher depending on your rig's weight. One practical note: Texas requires you to get your vehicle inspected, which means a trip to a Texas inspection station when the sticker is due.

**Florida** rounds out the top three. No state income tax, a warm climate that many RVers naturally pass through, and a large infrastructure of mail services. Establishing Florida domicile requires more steps than South Dakota, including proof of address documents and sometimes a Florida bank account. Registration fees vary but are generally competitive. One consideration: Florida's property insurance market is under serious strain, which matters less to a full-timer but can affect any real estate you own there.

## Other States Worth Considering

South Dakota, Texas, and Florida get the press, but they're not the only options.

**Wyoming** has no state income tax and is sometimes overlooked. Vehicle registration can be done by mail through county clerk offices, and the state has a reputation for being low-hassle. Mail forwarding services in Cody and Cheyenne cater to RVers specifically.

**Nevada** is another no-income-tax state that some full-timers choose, particularly those who spend significant time in the West. Vehicle registration fees are moderate, and Las Vegas has several mail forwarding operations. One potential downside: Nevada has stricter rules about physical presence to establish residency initially.

**Montana** is famous for one specific reason: LLC-based vehicle registration. Montana has no sales tax, which means registering an expensive motorhome or fifth wheel through a Montana LLC can save tens of thousands of dollars in sales tax that you'd otherwise pay in your home state. This strategy is controversial and some states (California, Colorado, and others) have begun actively pursuing RV owners who do this while physically living there for extended periods. It's a legal gray area that deserves separate research and probably a conversation with a tax attorney.

States with high income taxes, like California, New York, Oregon, and Illinois, are generally poor domicile choices for full-timers with significant income. These states also tend to be aggressive about claiming residency even after you've left.

## Step-by-Step: How to Establish Domicile in South Dakota

South Dakota's process is the most streamlined of the big three, which is why so many RVers start here. Here's how it actually works:

1. **Choose a mail forwarding service.** America's Mailbox (americasmailbox.com) and Alternative Resources are the two most established. Sign up before you arrive. They give you a physical street address (not a P.O. box) in South Dakota, which is required for your driver's license.

2. **Spend at least one night in South Dakota.** You'll need to show a receipt from a campground, hotel, or similar paid lodging. One night is the legal minimum.

3. **Visit the DMV.** Bring your lodging receipt, proof of mailing address from your forwarding service, your current license, and any required documentation (birth certificate, passport, Social Security card). The South Dakota DMV has same-day service at most locations.

4. **Register your vehicle.** You can do this at the same DMV visit or at the county treasurer's office. Bring your title, proof of insurance with South Dakota on it, and payment for registration fees.

5. **Update your voter registration.** This is optional but recommended. It reinforces your domicile intent and is a practical step for actually participating in elections.

6. **Close ties with your previous state.** Surrender your old driver's license when you get your South Dakota one. Change your address with banks, the IRS, Social Security, and any other institutions. Some people also open a South Dakota bank account for added documentation.

The whole process can genuinely be done in a single business day if you're organized. Many RVers plan a one-week South Dakota trip just to handle domicile, explore the Badlands, and enjoy the area before moving on.

## How Domicile Affects Insurance, Healthcare, and Taxes

Getting your license and registration in a new state is just the beginning. The downstream effects touch almost every area of your financial life.

**Health insurance** is one of the biggest surprises for new full-timers. If you're buying insurance on the Affordable Care Act marketplace, your premium is based on your domicile state. South Dakota plans tend to cost more per month than Texas plans, for example, but the networks and coverage areas differ significantly. If you travel across many states, you'll want a plan with a wide national network, which narrows your options considerably. Many full-timers end up with short-term health plans or health sharing ministries as a workaround, though both have limitations.

**Income taxes** are the headline issue. Moving your domicile to a no-income-tax state can save a remote worker earning $80,000 a year roughly $3,000 to $6,000 annually compared to staying domiciled in a state like Oregon (9.9% top rate) or California (up to 13.3%). The savings are real and significant over time.

**Vehicle registration** costs vary wildly. A 38-foot Class A diesel pusher might cost $600 a year to register in South Dakota, $1,800 in Florida, and over $2,500 in California. That gap adds up fast, especially if you're also registering a tow vehicle or toad.

**Jury duty** is an obligation that comes with domicile. You'll receive jury summonses at your mail forwarding address. Most states allow you to defer or be excused if you can't physically appear, but it's not something to ignore.

## Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

A few mistakes show up repeatedly with RV domicile decisions.

**Not actually cutting ties with your old state.** This is the most common and costly error. If your kids are still enrolled in school in California, if you're still receiving mail at a California address, or if you spend more than 183 days a year in a high-tax state, that state may assert you're still a resident. Keep travel logs, receipts, and calendar records. Document where you are.

**Choosing a domicile state based on one factor.** Someone hears "no income tax" and picks South Dakota without realizing their job has a health insurance plan tied to regional networks that doesn't work well there. Or they pick Florida without accounting for the hassle of vehicle inspections or the higher registration fees. Run the full numbers.

**Ignoring business structure.** If you run a business as a full-timer, your domicile state affects your business registration, and the two should generally align. A business registered in Wyoming while you're domiciled in Texas creates unnecessary complexity.

**Using a P.O. box.** Most states require a physical street address for driver's license and vehicle registration purposes. A mail forwarding service that provides a real street address is essential.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I stay domiciled in my home state if I go full-time?

Technically yes, if your home state allows it and you maintain the required ties. But if your home state has a high income tax rate, you're leaving money on the table. Most full-timers find that formally establishing domicile in a no-income-tax state is worth the effort within the first year.

### Do I have to physically visit my domicile state every year?

South Dakota has no minimum annual visit requirement. Texas and Florida also have no hard annual return requirement, though you're expected to maintain genuine intent to make the state your permanent home. Spending zero time there over several years can create documentation problems if your domicile is ever challenged.

### What address do I use for my domicile if I don't own property there?

A mail forwarding service provides a physical street address you can use for your driver's license, vehicle registration, and mailing address. Services like Americas Mailbox in South Dakota or Escapees' Rainbow's End location in Livingston, Texas, are built specifically for this purpose.

### Can two spouses have different domicile states?

Legally yes, but it's complicated. Married couples filing jointly typically share a domicile state for tax purposes. If spouses have different domicile states, it can create filing issues and potential audit triggers. Most couples choose one state and both establish domicile there together.

### What happens if I get pulled over in a state that's not my domicile state?

Nothing unusual. You present your license and registration from your domicile state, just like any out-of-state driver would. The issue only arises if an officer or state agency believes you've been living in their state long enough to require local registration, which is typically more than 90 days in most states.
