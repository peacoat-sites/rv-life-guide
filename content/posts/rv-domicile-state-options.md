You sold your house, stuffed everything into a 36-foot motorhome, and you're ready to go. Then someone asks: "So where do you *live*?" That question stops most new full-timers cold. The answer matters way more than you'd think. Your domicile state determines your income tax burden, vehicle registration costs, health insurance options, and even your jury duty obligations. Pick wrong and you could owe thousands in unexpected taxes. Pick right and you might save $4,000 to $8,000 a year or more, depending on your income.

## What Domicile Actually Means (And Why It's Not the Same as Residency)

People throw "domicile" and "residency" around like they're the same thing. They're not. Your residence is where you physically sleep. Your domicile is your permanent legal home, the place you intend to return to even if you're away for years. For full-time RVers, there's often no physical residence at all. Which is why choosing a domicile state is actually a choice you have to make.

Every state has its own rules about what creates domicile. Generally it requires getting a driver's license, registering your vehicle, and maintaining a mailing address in the state. Some states also want proof of banking, voter registration, or a notarized statement of intent. The bar varies, but hitting the basic requirements isn't usually the hard part. The hard part is choosing which state deserves that legal tie.

Here's the thing: your previous home state doesn't automatically release you just because you drove away. If you owned a home in California and you're still receiving mail there, California may still consider you a resident and expect income tax. You've got to formally establish domicile elsewhere and actively cut ties with your old state. It's not optional.

## The Big Three: South Dakota, Texas, and Florida

Three states dominate the RV domicile conversation. They're the most RV-friendly, they have established infrastructure for non-resident registration, and they've been doing this long enough that the process is well understood.

**South Dakota** is probably the most popular choice among full-time RVers. No state income tax. Vehicle registration fees are reasonable, around $270 for a larger motorhome annually (varies by weight and type). The state lets you establish domicile in a single day if you visit in person, and mail forwarding companies like America's Mailbox in Box Elder and Alternative Resources in Sioux Falls have processed thousands of RVer applications. You'll need a physical address for your driver's license and registration, which these services provide. South Dakota also has relatively lenient rules about how often you need to physically return to the state.

**Texas** is the other major contender. No state income tax either, and Texas has a large RV culture with mail forwarding services clustered around Livingston (home of the Escapees RV Club's headquarters). The Texas process requires more documentation than South Dakota, and vehicle registration fees can run higher depending on your rig's weight. One practical thing to know: Texas requires you to get your vehicle inspected, which means a trip to a Texas inspection station when the sticker is due.

**Florida** rounds out the top three. No state income tax, a warm climate that many RVers naturally pass through, and solid infrastructure of mail services. Establishing Florida domicile requires more steps than South Dakota, proof of address documents and sometimes a Florida bank account. Registration fees vary but are generally competitive. One thing to consider: Florida's property insurance market is under serious strain. That matters less to a full-timer but can affect any real estate you own there.

## Other States Worth Considering

South Dakota, Texas, and Florida get all the attention, but they're not the only options.

**Wyoming** has no state income tax and often gets overlooked. Vehicle registration can be done by mail through county clerk offices, and the state has a reputation for being low-hassle. Mail forwarding services in Cody and Cheyenne cater to RVers specifically.

**Nevada** is another no-income-tax state that some full-timers choose, particularly those who spend significant time in the West. Vehicle registration fees are moderate, and Las Vegas has several mail forwarding operations. One potential downside: Nevada has stricter rules about physical presence to establish residency initially.

**Montana** deserves mention for one specific reason: LLC-based vehicle registration. Montana has no sales tax, which means registering an expensive motorhome or fifth wheel through a Montana LLC can save tens of thousands of dollars in sales tax that you'd otherwise pay in your home state. This strategy is controversial. California, Colorado, and other states have started actively pursuing RV owners who do this while physically living there for extended periods. It's a legal gray area that deserves separate research and probably a conversation with a tax attorney before you try it.

States with high income taxes, California, New York, Oregon, Illinois, are generally poor domicile choices for full-timers with significant income. They're also aggressive about claiming residency even after you've left.

## Step-by-Step: How to Establish Domicile in South Dakota

South Dakota's process is the most streamlined of the big three. Here's how it actually works:

1. **Choose a mail forwarding service.** America's Mailbox (americasmailbox.com) and Alternative Resources are the two most established. Sign up before you arrive. They give you a physical street address (not a P.O. box) in South Dakota, which is required for your driver's license.

2. **Spend at least one night in South Dakota.** You'll need to show a receipt from a campground, hotel, or similar paid lodging. One night is the legal minimum.

3. **Visit the DMV.** Bring your lodging receipt, proof of mailing address from your forwarding service, your current license, and any required documentation (birth certificate, passport, Social Security card). The South Dakota DMV has same-day service at most locations.

4. **Register your vehicle.** You can do this at the same DMV visit or at the county treasurer's office. Bring your title, proof of insurance with South Dakota on it, and payment for registration fees.

5. **Update your voter registration.** This is optional but recommended. It reinforces your domicile intent and is a practical step for actually participating in elections.

6. **Close ties with your previous state.** Surrender your old driver's license when you get your South Dakota one. Change your address with banks, the IRS, Social Security, and any other institutions. Some people also open a South Dakota bank account for added documentation.

The whole process can genuinely happen in a single business day if you're organized. Many RVers plan a one-week South Dakota trip just to handle domicile, explore the Badlands, and enjoy the area before moving on.

## How Domicile Affects Insurance, Healthcare, and Taxes

Getting your license and registration in a new state is just the beginning. The downstream effects touch almost everything.

**Health insurance** hits a lot of new full-timers by surprise. If you're buying on the Affordable Care Act marketplace, your premium is based on your domicile state. South Dakota plans tend to cost more per month than Texas plans, but the networks and coverage areas differ significantly. Travel across many states and you'll want a plan with a wide national network, which narrows your options. Many full-timers end up with short-term health plans or health sharing ministries instead, though both have real limitations.

**Income taxes** are the headline issue. Moving your domicile to a no-income-tax state can save a remote worker earning $80,000 a year roughly $3,000 to $6,000 annually compared to staying domiciled in Oregon (9.9% top rate) or California (up to 13.3%). The savings compound over time.

**Vehicle registration** costs vary wildly. A 38-foot Class A diesel pusher might cost $600 a year to register in South Dakota, $1,800 in Florida, and over $2,500 in California. That gap adds up fast, especially if you're also registering a toad.

**Jury duty** is an obligation that comes with domicile. You'll receive summonses at your mail forwarding address. Most states allow you to defer or be excused if you can't physically appear, but it's not something to ignore.

A few mistakes show up over and over with RV domicile decisions.

**Not actually cutting ties with your old state.** This is the most common and costly error. If your kids are still enrolled in school in California, if you're still receiving mail at a California address, or if you spend more than 183 days a year in a high-tax state, that state may assert you're still a resident. Keep travel logs, receipts, and calendar records. Document where you are.

**Choosing a domicile state based on one factor.** Someone hears "no income tax" and picks South Dakota without realizing their job's health insurance plan is tied to regional networks that don't work there. Or they pick Florida without accounting for vehicle inspections or higher registration fees. Run the full numbers.

**Ignoring business structure.** If you run a business as a full-timer, your domicile state affects your business registration, and the two should generally align. A business registered in Wyoming while you're domiciled in Texas creates unnecessary complexity.

**Using a P.O. box.** Most states require a physical street address for driver's license and vehicle registration purposes. A mail forwarding service that provides a real street address is essential.
