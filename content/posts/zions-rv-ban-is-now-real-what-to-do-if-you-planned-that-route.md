You're halfway through planning your fall Southwest loop, Zion is on the route, and you just found out the tunnel permit you were counting on doesn't exist anymore. Not delayed, not modified. Gone. That's the situation thousands of [RVers are](/why-rvers-are-winning-the-dust-off-summer-of-2026/) waking up to right now, and if your rig is on the larger side, the route you built your trip around just got erased.

Effective June 7, 2026, Zion [National Park](/national-park-rv-camping/) began enforcing a hard prohibition on oversized vehicles along the entire Zion-Mt. Carmel Highway between Canyon Junction and the East Entrance. We're not talking about the tunnel specifically anymore. The whole corridor is off-limits if your rig exceeds 11'4" tall, 7'10" wide, 35'9" long, or 50,000 lbs. The old paid-escort system through the tunnel, the one you might remember from past trips or saw mentioned in older trip reports, has been permanently discontinued. As RV LIFE reported on June 8, 2026, this affects most Class A and Class C motorhomes, fifth wheels, dually trucks, and trailers with more than 26 feet from hitch to rear axle. That's most of us.

The why actually matters here. Studies found the escort procedure was blocking traffic for an average of 41 minutes out of every hour during peak season. Some hours had just 8 minutes of free-flowing traffic. Zion is the second-most visited national park in the country, with visitation roughly 90 times higher than it was in 1930 when that highway was built. The old system was unsustainable. The park made a call. Fair or not, it's real.

## Does Your Rig Actually Qualify as Oversized?

This is where people get tripped up. The restrictions sound clear until you start measuring and realize your "35-foot" [fifth wheel](/fifth-wheel-vs-travel-trailer/) is measured differently depending on who's doing the measuring. For the Zion restriction, the 35'9" length applies to the total vehicle, and CamperFAQs noted in their June 6, 2026 coverage that trailers with more than 26 feet from hitch to rear axle are specifically captured under the rule. The width limit of 7'10" catches a lot of dually trucks even without a trailer. And the 11'4" height limit? That grabs the majority of Class A coaches, many of which run 12'6" or taller with rooftop AC units.

If you're not sure, measure before you go. I've seen people drive six hours to find out their rig doesn't fit somewhere, and it's a brutal feeling. Check your coach's actual roof height including any antenna or AC shroud, not the chassis spec sheet. The spec sheet lies.

## The Reroute Is Not Actually That Bad

Here's what most people don't realize: the alternate route isn't the ordeal it sounds like. If you're coming from Page, Arizona or heading toward Bryce Canyon, the reroute via US-89, AZ-389, and UT-59 into Hurricane, Utah adds somewhere between 10 and 45 minutes depending on exactly where you're headed. That's it. You're not adding two hours. You're adding a coffee stop.

What you do lose is the scenic drive through the tunnel corridor and the eastern canyon views from the highway itself. That's a real loss, especially if you've never driven it. But you can still access Zion's main canyon from the south and west. The restriction is east-side only. Springdale is still reachable. The visitor center, the shuttle system, Angels Landing permits, the Narrows, all of that is still on the table. You're just coming in from the other direction.

## Replanning Your Southwest Loop

If Zion was a waypoint on a longer route, say a classic Utah five-parks loop or a southbound run from Salt Lake to the Grand Canyon, the reroute actually connects well with Hurricane and St. George on the west side. I've run that corridor multiple times and there's solid RV infrastructure there. Quail Creek State Park outside St. George has decent hookup sites. Zion River Resort in Virgin is popular and well-reviewed. If you're booked at a campground east of the park thinking you'd enter from that side, you may need to reconsider your campsite entirely.

| Scenario | Impact | Practical Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Booked at Zion Ponderosa or east-side camp | Can't drive your rig to site from west | Rebook on west side or tow a smaller tow vehicle |
| Doing a north-south corridor run | 10–45 min reroute via US-89/AZ-389/UT-59 | Minor, plan extra fuel stop in Kanab |
| Relying on east entrance for Checkerboard Mesa access | No oversized vehicle access at all | Hike from shuttle or use personal vehicle |
| Pulling a tow vehicle behind Class A | Whole rig length almost certainly exceeds limit | Unhitch tow car, drive personal vehicle in separately |

If you're towing a car behind a Class A, that combination almost certainly pushes you over the length limit. The good news: you can unhitch at a campground on the west side and drive your personal vehicle into the east sections for a day trip. That's genuinely how a lot of full-timers handle park access anyway.

## What the iRV2 Community Is Saying

The iRV2 forums thread on this, posted back in January 2026, has been active all year and is worth reading if you want real-world reports from people who've already run the reroute this summer. The general consensus is that the restriction enforcement is real and consistent, rangers are checking at the Canyon Junction turnoff, and people who tried to "just see if they'd get stopped" did, in fact, get stopped. Don't test it. The fine is not worth the story.

What most people don't realize, based on that thread, is that the signage on the approach is actually better than expected. The park has put up clear warnings well before the restriction point, which gives you time to turn around without doing it in an awkward spot. Small comfort, but it's something.

The frustration is legitimate. A lot of people built multi-week trips around this route, and finding out mid-planning that the corridor you assumed was accessible just isn't anymore is genuinely annoying. But the reroute works, the park is still spectacular, and if anything, the west entrance is less chaotic than the tunnel backup used to be. I've sat in that tunnel queue for over an hour before. Whatever else this change does, it ends that particular misery.
