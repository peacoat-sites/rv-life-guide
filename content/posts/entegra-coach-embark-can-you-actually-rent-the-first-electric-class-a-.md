Most people assume the first mainstream electric Class A RV is still a few years out, something being promised at trade shows but not yet real enough to touch. That assumption is now wrong. The Entegra Coach Embark entered full commercial production in 2026, and [right now](/why-right-now-is-a-rare-buyers-market-for-rvs/), today, you can rent one through THL before it ever hits a dealer lot. That's not a soft launch or a press preview. That's a working rig in a rental fleet, available to actual RVers, while Entegra collects feedback before finalizing the product for public sale. I've been watching this one closely, and the rental strategy alone tells you something interesting about where the company's head is at.

The Embark is built on an EV platform developed exclusively by Harbinger Motors, which is worth paying attention to because this isn't a converted diesel chassis with a battery pack bolted on. According to THOR Industries' September 2025 press release, this is a purpose-built electric architecture, and it shows in the specs. You get 105 miles of pure electric range, plus a gasoline range extender that brings total range up to 450 miles. That's a number that matters. Most existing EV RVs average around 125 miles total. The Embark's 450-mile combined figure puts it in a completely different conversation, one where you can actually plan a trip without spreadsheet anxiety.

GearJunkie's first-look review from September 2025 highlighted the 800-volt architecture as a particular standout, noting that it supports rapid DC fast charging in a way that lower-voltage platforms simply can't match. That 800V system is also what enables the Embark's vehicle-to-home capability, meaning this rig can serve as emergency backup power for a house. I'll be honest, that feature probably sounds like a press release bullet point until you're sitting in hurricane country watching your neighbors' generators run dry.

## Why the Rental-First Strategy Isn't What It Looks Like

At first glance, making a Class A available to rent before it's for sale sounds like a marketing play. I don't think that's the whole story. Entegra is using THL's rental fleet as a real-world test environment, collecting consumer data on how actual RVers use the rig, not how engineers imagine they will. Eight years of living in rigs has taught me that the gap between those two things is massive. How people manage charge stops, how they use [shore power](/new-shore-power-safety-rule-is-coming-for-all-new-rvs/) overnight, whether they're lean on the generator or heavy, all of that is genuinely different from lab assumptions. This rental period is smart engineering, not just clever PR.

What surprised me was how narrow the window is. Units are in rental now, retail is expected in late 2026 or early 2027, and pricing is projected between $300,000 and $400,000. That means the feedback loop is tight. Entegra isn't collecting two years of data. They're collecting months.

## The Range Question, Answered Honestly

The number one objection to EV RVs has always been range anxiety, and it's legitimate. A 125-mile ceiling isn't workable for full-[time travel](/best-rv-trip-planning-app-for-full-time-travel/). Period. The Embark's range extender changes the math in a way that previous EV RVs couldn't. Here's how it compares to what's been available:

| Vehicle | Electric-Only Range | Total Range | Architecture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entegra Coach Embark | 105 miles | 450 miles | 800V, range-extended EV |
| Winnebago eRV2 | ~125 miles | ~125 miles | Standard EV, no extender |
| Thor Vision Vehicle (concept) | Not disclosed | Not disclosed | Concept only, no production |

The range extender doesn't make this a hybrid in the traditional sense. It's an EV that carries a gasoline backup specifically to eliminate the worst-case scenario. For full-timers doing long travel days, that's the architecture that actually works in practice. You'd run electric on short hops and around town, fire up the extender for big mileage days, and still arrive with more flexibility than any diesel rig gives you in terms of fuel stop timing.

## What $300,000-$400,000 Actually Buys You

I'll be honest about something: that price range is brutal. Even at the low end, $300,000 puts the Embark well above most luxury diesel pushers from the same era. For context, a fully loaded Entegra Aspire or Cornerstone in 2025 was landing in the $350,000-$450,000 range, so the Embark isn't wildly out of step with top-tier Class A pricing. But you're still paying a premium for being early, and early adopters in this segment always absorb the development cost.

The calculation shifts if you factor in fuel. A full-time RVer pulling 25,000-30,000 miles a year in a diesel Class A is spending real money on fuel, often $10,000-$15,000 annually depending on routes and diesel prices. Running the Embark primarily on electricity, with the range extender only kicking in for big days, compresses that number meaningfully. Over five years, the math starts to look less absurd, though I'd want to see real-world consumption data from the rental fleet before I'd bet hard on any specific figure.

## The Industry Context Nobody Is Talking About

The RV industry is not in great shape right now. According to Camper Report's July 2026 roundup of shipment data, overall RV shipments are down 14.2% through the first half of 2026 compared to 2025. Dealers are sitting on inventory. Manufacturers are cutting production. Against that backdrop, the Embark is one of the few things generating genuine forward momentum in the industry rather than defensive headlines. Whether it translates to sales volume is a different question. At $300,000-$400,000, this is not a rig that moves in large numbers. But it signals something about where THOR Industries thinks the category is going, and that signal matters to everyone watching the space.

The research here is mixed on whether the broader EV RV market will accelerate quickly or slowly. Charging infrastructure outside of major corridors is still thin. The full-time RVer who boondocks heavily has a different problem set than someone doing campground-to-campground travel with shore power available nightly. The Embark's architecture handles the second scenario very well and the first scenario better than anything else currently available, but "better than the competition" and "genuinely solves the problem" aren't always the same thing.

Renting the Embark through THL before the retail window opens is a genuinely unusual opportunity. If you're even remotely curious about whether electric Class A travel is workable for your specific style of RVing, this is the moment to find out before committing to a quarter-million-dollar decision. That's not hype. That's just good timing.
