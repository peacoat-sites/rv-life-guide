You pull up to Zion's East Entrance on a Tuesday morning in July, towing your fifth wheel, and the ranger walks up to your window with that look. You know the one. "Sir, I'm going to need you to turn around." Behind you, a line of RVs stretches back half a mile, everyone equally confused, because nobody told them the tunnel permit system they've been using for years is gone. Permanently. As of June 7, 2026, Zion [National Park](/national-park-rv-camping/) banned oversized vehicles from the entire Zion-Mt. Carmel Highway, and I've been watching the fallout roll through RV forums and Facebook groups all summer.

This isn't a temporary COVID-era closure or a seasonal restriction. The National Park Service made this permanent, backed by two safety studies from 1989 and 2019, and validated by the Federal Highway Administration. The highway is a 1930s-era two-lane road with a dozen-plus sharp curves, narrow lanes, and steep drop-offs. The FHWA looked at it and basically said: yeah, those big rigs were never really safe there to begin with. The old tunnel permit workaround, the one that let oversized rigs through with a ranger escort during limited hours for a fee, is done. The RVIA's Government Affairs team is actively pushing the NPS for clearer messaging on this, which tells you everything you need to know about how many people are showing up uninformed [right now](/why-right-now-is-a-rare-buyers-market-for-rvs/), according to RVIA's February 2026 announcement about the change.

What most people don't realize is that this ban covers the entire highway between Canyon Junction and the East Entrance, not just the tunnel itself. That's a critical distinction. You're not getting stopped at the tunnel portal. You're getting turned away before you even get to the good part.

## The Exact Limits, and Why Most Big Rigs Fail at Least One

The four thresholds are specific, and the "or" in that sentence matters a lot. Your rig doesn't have to fail all four. One is enough.

| Measurement | Maximum Allowed |
| --- | --- |
| Length | 35 ft 9 in |
| Width | 7 ft 10 in |
| Height | 11 ft 4 in |
| Gross Weight | 50,000 lbs |

A standard Class A diesel pusher runs 40 to 45 feet. A lot of fifth-wheel-and-truck combos hit 55 to 60 feet combined. Even a mid-size Class A at 36 feet is already over the length limit. And width catches people off guard: many Class A coaches are 102 inches (8 ft 6 in) wide before you add slide rooms or mirrors. The height limit is the one that kills cargo vans and smaller Class B+ rigs that added a roof AC unit. If you're in a 30-foot Class C with a standard cab-over, you might actually be fine, but you need to measure everything, not guess.

## What You Can Still Do (and It's Actually Not Bad)

Here's the thing nobody leading with the doom-and-gloom coverage mentions: Zion is still fully accessible to oversized rigs. You just come in from the other side. The South Entrance at Springdale, reached via I-15 and SR-9, completely bypasses the tunnel and the Mt. Carmel Highway. You park in Springdale, hop the shuttle, and you're in the park. That's how most visitors experience Zion anyway. The shuttle system to Angels Landing, the Narrows trailhead, Emerald Pools, all of it runs from Springdale.

I'll be honest: the South Entrance approach is less dramatic than coming in through the moonscape of the east side. Rolling through that tunnel with the canyon suddenly opening up below you is one of those moments in RV travel you don't forget. Losing that approach is a real loss for big-rig travelers. But the park itself is very much still there, and the shuttle means you're not fighting for parking inside the canyon anyway.

If you're specifically trying to do the scenic drive from the east, coming in through Mt. Carmel Junction from US-89, you need to be in a smaller rig. A Class B, a small Class C, a lightweight [travel trailer](/fifth-wheel-vs-travel-trailer/). Plan accordingly or plan to enter from Springdale.

## Campground Reality Check for This Summer

The campgrounds near the South Entrance are going to be under real pressure this summer. Watchman Campground and South Campground inside the park have hookup and tent sites, but they book through recreation.gov months out. I've seen people assume they'll figure out camping when they get there and end up driving 40 minutes to find anything with availability.

Settler's Point Luxury RV Resort and similar private parks in Hurricane and St. George are picking up overflow, and they're showing as heavily booked through August, based on availability checks as of late June 2026. If you're heading to Zion in July or August 2026 without a reservation somewhere, you're gambling. Don't gamble. The closest full-hookup options to Springdale that can handle big rigs are mostly 20 to 30 minutes out, which is manageable, but you need to book now.

## How to Check Your Rig Before You Go

Pull your manufacturer's spec sheet, not the listing description you saw when you bought it. Manufacturers measure width without mirrors in many cases. Add your mirrors and slides to get real-world width. For length on a fifth wheel, measure from the nose of the trailer to the rear bumper, then add the truck separately for the combined length question (though the restriction is specifically on vehicle length, confirm with the current NPS guidance at the East Entrance gate or UDOT's SR-9 restrictions page before you go).

The NPS is not sending you a warning email. The East Entrance ranger is not going to wave you through as a courtesy. According to RV LIFE's June 8, 2026 coverage of the change, rigs exceeding the limits will not be permitted past Canyon Junction. That's a hard stop, not a negotiation.

The time to figure out whether your rig clears those four thresholds is right now, at home, before you've got three days of food in the fridge and a kid asking how much longer until they see a canyon.
