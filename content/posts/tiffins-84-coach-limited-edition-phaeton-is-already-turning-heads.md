Tiffin Motorhomes dropped a significant announcement on November 10, 2026: a limited-edition Class A diesel pusher built not to chase a market trend, but to honor a person. The Bob Tiffin Limited Edition Phaeton exists because Bob Tiffin turned 84, and the company he founded decided 84 coaches was exactly the right number to commemorate that. It's a collector-grade release in a segment that rarely uses that phrase honestly, and if you follow the luxury motorhome space, you've probably already seen the RV News video walkthrough that published November 20, 2026, and lit up the forums.

This isn't a press-release coach dressed up with badge trim. The production cap is real and hard: 42 units for model year 2027, 42 for model year 2028, each individually numbered with an engraved plaque and Bob Tiffin's embroidered signature on the front seats. That's it. When those 84 are gone, they're gone. For full-timers and serious luxury buyers, the question isn't really whether this coach is impressive. It's whether it's the right fit for your situation, what it'll actually cost you to own one, and whether the collectibility argument holds any water in the real world.

According to RV Pro's coverage from November 10, 2026, the Limited Edition Phaeton is built on Tiffin's PowerGlide XC raised-rail, rear-engine diesel chassis with a 450-horsepower Cummins L9 under the hood. Two floorplans are available: the 40 RTH and the 44 RTH. The exterior paint options, Cinderwood, Nasa, and Waterfall, draw from Rocky Mountain landscapes and include custom classic-car-tribute detailing you won't find on the standard Phaeton line.

## What You're Actually Getting Under the Hood

The Cummins L9 at 450 horsepower is a proven unit. Cummins has built L9-series engines for commercial and RV applications for years, and owner communities on forums like iRV2 have logged high mileage on them with proper maintenance intervals. The PowerGlide XC raised-rail chassis gives the coach a lower center of gravity and improved handling dynamics compared to standard rail setups, which matters on mountain roads, the same terrain that inspired the paint schemes. If you're planning western routes, that's not marketing fluff.

What you don't yet have is a confirmed MSRP for the Limited Edition specifically. The standard 2026 Tiffin Phaeton 44 OH retails for $652,957 according to current pricing, and that's before you factor in the exclusivity premium, the engraved hardware, or the signature detailing. Budget conservatively north of that number. Dealers won't be discounting a production run of 42 coaches [per year](/how-much-to-budget-for-rv-maintenance-per-year/).

| Engine | Cummins L9, 450 hp | Cummins L9, 450 hp |

| Target Buyer | Campgrounds with length limits | Full-timers wanting max space |

The 44 RTH gives you more living space and storage, which full-timers typically want. But 44 feet creates real campground access problems. Many [state park](/california-just-banned-ghost-campers-here-is-what-rvers-need-to-know/) loops cap at 35 or 40 feet. If you're serious about boondocking or mixing in state parks with private RV resorts, the 40 RTH is worth the smaller footprint. The 44 RTH makes more sense if you're mostly staying in full-hookup Class A-friendly resorts where site length is rarely a constraint.

## Collectibility: Real or Hype?

The honest answer is: probably both. Numbered limited editions from established manufacturers do hold secondary market value better than standard production units, particularly when the production count is verifiable and low. The engraved plaque and embroidered signature are physical evidence of provenance, not just a sticker on the door. That matters to buyers down the road.

What collectors and flippers sometimes miss is that motorhomes depreciate on a curve that's steep in the first three years regardless of exclusivity. A coach bought today and kept in storage to preserve resale value is a coach you're not living in, which defeats the point. If you're buying it to use, buy it because it's exceptional on its merits, the chassis, the engine, the finish quality. The collectibility story is a secondary benefit, not a primary investment thesis.

The Camper Report's November 13, 2026, roundup noted the broader industry attention this release is getting, which signals that resale visibility will be higher than average. That helps. But treat it as a bonus, not a guarantee.

## What Ownership Actually Costs

Before writing the check or signing the financing paperwork, run the numbers on what this coach [costs per](/rv-fuel-costs-per-mile/) year beyond the purchase price. Diesel Class A coaches at this size and weight average somewhere between 7 and 10 miles per gallon depending on terrain, speed, and load. Cross-country full-timing at current diesel prices adds up fast. Insurance on a coach approaching or exceeding $700,000 MSRP will be significantly higher than a standard Class A policy. Storage, if you aren't a full-timer, is a real line item.

The [Cost Ownership Calc](/rv-cost-of-ownership-calculator/) can help you add up the true yearly cost of owning and running a coach at this price point before the payment shock hits. It's worth doing before you fall in love with the paint.

Maintenance on the Cummins L9 follows Cummins' published service intervals: oil changes typically every 15,000 to 25,000 miles depending on oil type and operating conditions, per Cummins' own L9 service documentation. Factor in that chassis service on a raised-rail rear-engine diesel isn't a quick-lube situation. You'll need a dealer or qualified diesel shop with the equipment to handle a coach this size.

## Who Should Actually Buy One

Full-timers who already know the Phaeton platform and want the best version of it. Luxury buyers who plan to log serious miles on it, not park it. People who understand that at $650,000-plus you're buying a coach that needs to earn its place in your budget through use, not appreciation. And anyone who wants something they can point to and say, with documentation, that it's one of 84 ever made.

If you're newer to Class A diesel ownership and this would be your first coach, the Limited Edition Phaeton is probably the wrong starting point. Learn the platform on a standard Phaeton first. The learning curve on any 40-to-44-foot diesel pusher is real, campground check-ins, backing into sites, weight distribution, water and tank management, and you don't want to work through that curve in a coach this rare.

The 84-unit run will sell. That part isn't really in question. The question is whether you're the right buyer for one of those 84 slots, and that's a decision worth making with your eyes open and your spreadsheet honest.
