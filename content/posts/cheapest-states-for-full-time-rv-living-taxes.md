Most full-time RVers pick their [domicile state](/rv-domicile-state-options/) the same way they pick a campsite: based on whoever they talked to last. "Texas is the way to go." "No, South Dakota's better." "My buddy did Florida and loves it." That's not a strategy. That's campfire advice, and it costs people real money.

The short version: three states dominate the domicile conversation for good reason, a couple of others are underrated, and a few popular choices are traps. The differences aren't trivial. Pick the wrong state and you could be paying $600 to $900 more per year just in vehicle registration fees, plus income tax exposure if you're not careful about where your money originates.

## The Tax Picture Most Guides Miss

Everyone leads with income tax. No state income tax equals a win. Fine, but that's only part of it.

The real money is often in your rig's annual registration and any personal property tax your state of domicile levies on vehicles. South Carolina charges a property tax on RVs that can run $1,200 to $2,400 a year depending on rig value, even though the state income tax rate is relatively low. Tennessee has no income tax on wages but does tax investment income, which matters if you're retired and living off a portfolio. Nevada looks clean on paper until you try to actually establish residency without a local mailing service, which is harder there than in [South Dakota](/south-dakota-rv-domicile/).

A reader named Cliff, who'd been full-timing for four years out of Nevada, emailed me after reading an older version of this topic. He'd been paying a [mail forwarding service](/best-mail-forwarding-service-for-full-time-rvers/) $240 a year and still getting hassled every renewal cycle about proving residency. He switched to South Dakota. The whole process cost him one overnight stay at a Sioux Falls budget motel and a morning at the DMV.

## The Actual Rankings

Here's where I'll commit to something most articles won't: a ranked order, not a "they're all great, it depends" hedge.

**1. South Dakota.** The fastest, cheapest, most RV-friendly setup in the country. No state income tax. No vehicle property tax. Registration is based on weight, not value, so a $150,000 diesel pusher doesn't get punished the way it would in other states. You can establish residency in a single day using one of the Sioux Falls mail forwarding services (America's Mailbox and My Dakota Address are the two I've seen most RVers use). The clerk at the DMV will ask you if you want to register your toad vehicle at the same time, and the answer is almost always yes.

**2. Texas.** No income tax, and registration fees are reasonable, though not as low as South Dakota's. The catch: Texas requires you to get a vehicle inspection every year, which is a minor hassle when you're 1,400 miles away from Texas come renewal time. Some inspection shops near RV parks in the Houston and San Antonio areas know the drill and service out-of-state domicile folks regularly, so it's manageable. Texas does not charge personal property tax on RVs registered as vehicles (as opposed to real property), which is the right call.

**3. Florida.** Solid choice, especially if you spend winters in the state anyway. No income tax. Registration fees are mid-range. The reason Florida drops to third is that it's harder to establish initial residency remotely, and annual registration renewals can feel bureaucratic compared to South Dakota's clean system. Still a strong option.

The others you'll hear about: Wyoming (no income tax, reasonable fees, but thin infrastructure for establishing domicile), Montana (popular for LLC-based RV title strategies to avoid sales tax, which is legally gray and not what this article is addressing), Nevada (no income tax but a pain in practice for full-timers).

| South Dakota | None | None | ~$147 | Excellent (one day) |
| Texas | None | None | ~$238 | Good (inspection req'd) |
| Florida | None | None | ~$271 | Moderate |
| Wyoming | None | None | ~$214 | Fair (thin infrastructure) |

I'll be honest: I don't have solid numbers on Wyoming's remote residency rejection rate or how often Nevada DMV pushes back on mail-service addresses. What I can say is that every Wyoming domicile RVer I've talked to set it up in person during a run through the state, not remotely.

## What "Establishing Domicile" Actually Costs You

This is where people get blindsided. It's not just the state fees. It's the whole setup.

When I made the switch to South Dakota residency back in my second year on the road, I budgeted for the DMV. I didn't budget for new insurance quotes (rates vary by domicile state, sometimes significantly), the cost of a mail forwarding service ($100 to $200 a year), the one night of lodging in Sioux Falls, or the fact that I had to update my address with about 40 different entities over the following six weeks. Banks, brokerage accounts, Medicare or health insurance if applicable, credit cards, voter registration.

Some worked examples from real situations:

Cliff switching Nevada to South Dakota: paid $147 registration vs. $289 previously, saved $240 on mail service hassle, and his insurance rate dropped $18/month because South Dakota has lower uninsured motorist rates baked into actuarial tables. Annual net savings: roughly $794.

A couple I know, Ray and Donna out of a 2021 Tiffin Allegro Bus, were based in South Carolina. Their rig valued at $280,000 was generating about $2,100 a year in property tax. They shifted domicile to South Dakota. First-year total cost of the switch, including the drive to Sioux Falls, motel, DMV fees, and new plates: $612. They recovered that in four months.

Someone who earns $80,000/year in freelance income and moves from California domicile to Texas domicile: California's 9.3% marginal rate on income above ~$66,000 means they were paying around $1,300 in state tax on that upper tranche. Gone. That's the scenario where domicile choice makes the biggest single-year dollar difference.

## The Insurance Angle Nobody Talks About

Your domicile state directly affects your RV insurance premium. Not by 2 or 3 percent. I've seen 15 to 22 percent swings.

States with high uninsured motorist rates and dense population (Florida, notably) tend to produce higher premiums even for full-timers who are stateless in practice. South Dakota, with its low traffic density and claim history, tends to produce lower premiums. When I've called insurers to quote with different domicile states (yes, I've done this, more than once, out of curiosity and because I had a reader ask), the difference between a Florida and South Dakota address on the same rig has been as high as $340/year.

Progressive and National General both write full-timer policies. Good Sam Insurance is popular but I've found their rates competitive only on certain rig types. Get at least three quotes and run them with your actual domicile address, not a placeholder.
