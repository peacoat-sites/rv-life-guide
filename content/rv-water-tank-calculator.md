The moment you leave a campground with full hookups and head into dry camping territory, water management becomes the central logistics challenge of your trip. How long your fresh water lasts determines how far you can go and how long you can stay. How fast your gray and black tanks fill determines when you need to move or dump. Getting this calculation wrong leads to the kind of trip-ruining moments we have all experienced: running out of water on day two of a planned five-day boondock, or cutting a remote stay short because the gray tank overflowed.

We have been living full-time in our fifth wheel since 2019, and water management is still one of the skills we refine constantly. The numbers below come from hard-won experience, not textbook theory.

## How Water Use Adds Up

The biggest variable in your daily water budget is almost always showers. A normal five-minute shower uses about 7.5 gallons. With two people taking normal showers every day, that is 15 gallons just for showers - enough to drain a 60-gallon tank in four days if you are not careful about everything else. The navy shower protocol, which means water on for 30 seconds to get wet, off while soaping up, then on briefly to rinse, brings that down to about 3 gallons per person. That single habit change can double your boondocking range.

Drinking and cooking use roughly a gallon per person per day. Dishwashing uses about 2 gallons per day for a family, especially if you are scraping plates well before washing. Handwashing adds another half-gallon per person. If you have a composting toilet, you eliminate black tank concerns entirely and also save the 0.5 gallons per flush your standard RV toilet uses.

## Reading Your Results

The calculator shows four key numbers: daily water usage, and how many days until your fresh tank empties, your gray tank fills, and your black tank fills. The limiting factor is the constraint that determines how long you can actually stay in one spot.

For most setups, the gray tank fills before the fresh tank empties, because not all of your water consumption goes to gray (some is consumed as drinking water). This is why many experienced boondockers carry extra fresh water in Jerry cans while their gray tank is the actual duration limiter.

Black tank timing depends heavily on toilet type and habits. With a composting toilet, this is never a concern. With a standard RV toilet flushing four times per person per day at half a gallon per flush, a 30-gallon black tank lasts about 15 days for two people - usually longer than your fresh tank in conservation mode.

## Tank Size Realities by Rig Type

Travel trailers and small fifth wheels often have 30-40 gallon fresh tanks, which makes water conservation essential for anything beyond a two-day dry camp. Larger fifth wheels and Class A motorhomes commonly have 80-100 gallon fresh tanks, giving you much more flexibility. Class B vans and truck campers are on the tight end, sometimes with as little as 20-30 gallons.

If your rig has smaller tanks and you want to extend your boondocking range, external water storage is your best option. Collapsible 7-gallon water containers are easy to carry and stack, and many experienced boondockers carry 20-30 gallons of supplemental water when heading into a long stay at a remote site.

## Tips That Actually Move the Needle

Beyond navy showers, the practices that make the biggest difference: use paper plates for one meal per day to eliminate dishwashing, keep a small hand-sanitizer dispenser at the door to reduce handwashing, use a spray bottle for rinsing dishes before the actual wash, and run the kitchen faucet at the minimum flow needed rather than full blast.

If you have a gray tank issue before your fresh water runs out, you can drain gray into a portable container for disposal, extending your stay without moving the rig. We carry a 15-gallon blue portable tank for exactly this situation.

Water management is the skill that separates the campers from the boondockers. Once you understand your rig's actual numbers and build the habits around them, you can stay off-grid longer and with far less stress.
